## What a rewire really means

A full rewire replaces the cables, sockets, switches, light fittings and the consumer unit (the fuse board) throughout your home. It's the electrical equivalent of replacing the plumbing behind the walls rather than just swapping a tap.

It isn't always all-or-nothing, though. Sometimes a property only needs a partial rewire or a fuse board upgrade to bring the worst of it up to scratch. The honest answer on which one you need depends on the state of what's already in the walls, and that's something I check in person rather than guess at over the phone.

## Signs your wiring may be on its way out

Older Gloucester homes, especially solid-wall period terraces and village properties, often still carry wiring that was installed decades ago for a very different kind of household. Back then nobody was running an air fryer, a games console, a hot tub and an EV charger off the same circuits.

Ask yourself these before anything else:

- **Rubber, fabric or lead-sheathed cables.** If the wiring looks cloth-covered or perished, it predates modern PVC and is a genuine concern.
- **A fuse board with old rewireable fuses** (the ceramic holders with fuse wire) rather than modern breakers.
- **Trip switches that keep tripping**, or that need resetting to boil a kettle or run the oven.
- **Scorch marks, buzzing sockets, or a warm or fishy smell** near outlets.
- **Not enough sockets**, so the house runs on a web of extension leads.
- **No earthing or bonding** to taps, pipes and the gas supply.

None of these on its own proves you need a full rewire. Together they're a strong hint that your system is tired and worth a proper look.

## Why period properties need extra care

Older, solid-wall properties need careful, experienced handling and the right materials for their age. Chasing cables into lime-plastered walls and original lath is not the same job as hacking into modern plasterboard, and treating it the same way is how you end up with cracked plaster, crumbling render and a repair bill nobody warned you about.

This is the boring stuff before the pretty stuff, and it's exactly where a lot of national firms cut corners. I'd rather take the time to route cables sensibly and make good properly than leave you with a tidy circuit and a wrecked wall.

## Rewire or EICR first?

If you're not sure how bad things are, an EICR (Electrical Installation Condition Report) is the sensible starting point. It's a formal inspection that grades the faults in your system and tells you, in writing, what's dangerous, what's a concern, and what's fine. I'm City and Guilds 2391 qualified for this testing and inspection work, so you get a straight assessment rather than a sales pitch for a rewire you might not need.

For landlords, this isn't optional. A rented home in England must have its electrics inspected and tested at least every five years. If an EICR flags serious issues, that's often when a partial or full rewire comes into the conversation.

## Common questions

### How long does a rewire take?

It depends on the size of the property and whether it's partial or full, and whether you're living in it while the work happens. I'll give you a realistic timeframe when I quote, not a guess.

### Can I stay in the house during a rewire?

Often yes, especially for a partial job, though there'll be periods without power to certain circuits. We work around you as much as the job allows.

### Do I definitely need a full rewire if my fuse board is old?

Not always. Sometimes a consumer unit upgrade and a few remedial fixes are enough. An inspection tells us for certain, which is why I never quote a rewire sight unseen.

### Will I get certification?

Yes. The work is tested and signed off, so you have the paperwork you'll need for a sale, a mortgage or, for landlords, your legal obligations.
