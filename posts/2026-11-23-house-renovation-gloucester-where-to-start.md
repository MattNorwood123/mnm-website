## Start with the boring stuff before the pretty stuff

Everyone wants to talk about the kitchen island and the big bi-fold doors. The honest answer is that the jobs that protect your budget are the unglamorous ones you can't see once the plaster's on.

**Sort these first:**

- The roof and anything letting water in
- Old or unsafe wiring and the fuse board
- Dodgy plumbing, especially before new kitchens and bathrooms
- Damp, especially in older solid-wall homes

When the room is already stripped back, fixing these costs a fraction of what it does once your shiny new finish is sitting on top of them. Chase a leak or a cracked tile later and you're paying twice. If a roof check is on your list, summer is actually the sensible window, which I explain in [why summer is the best time for a roof inspection](/blog/summer-roof-inspection-gloucester/).

## Know what kind of house you're working with

Gloucester is full of older terraces and village period homes, and they don't behave like a modern new-build. Solid-wall properties need careful, experienced handling and proper materials made for their period. Lime plaster and lime mortar, for example, let these walls breathe. Seal them up with the wrong modern products and you can trap moisture and cause damp that shows up months down the line.

This is one of the most common things I see rushed. If your home is older, the materials matter as much as the workmanship.

## Decide the order of works before anyone lifts a tool

A renovation that jumps around costs more and takes longer. A sensible sequence usually looks like this:

1. **Structural and weatherproofing** — roof, walls, windows, anything keeping the weather out
2. **First fix** — plumbing and electrics run through the bones of the house
3. **Plastering and flooring** — getting surfaces ready
4. **Second fix** — fitting kitchens, bathrooms, sockets, radiators
5. **Finishes** — the decorating, tiling and the pretty stuff you'll actually show people

The biggest budget killer isn't tiles or taps. It's changing your mind halfway through. Every late decision means ripping out work you've already paid for, so lock down your layout and your choices early.

## Where renovation budgets actually blow

Ask yourself these before you commit to a single quote:

- Have I decided the full layout, or am I still "seeing how it looks"?
- Is the wiring and plumbing up to the job, or will it need replacing anyway?
- Does my older house need breathable materials I haven't budgeted for?
- Am I getting one clear quote, or lots of separate trades I'll have to chase?

That last point matters more than people think. Hire a plumber, an electrician, a plasterer and a roofer separately and you become the project manager, chasing diaries and sorting the gaps between them. Using one in-house team under a single point of contact removes most of that headache.
