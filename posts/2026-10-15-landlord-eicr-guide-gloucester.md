## What an EICR actually is

EICR stands for Electrical Installation Condition Report. In plain English, it's a thorough health check of the fixed electrical system in a property: the wiring, the consumer unit (the fuse board), the sockets, the switches, and the earthing and bonding that keep everything safe.

It is not a quick glance. A proper EICR means testing circuits, checking connections, and recording the condition of the installation against current safety standards. At the end you get a report that either confirms the installation is satisfactory or flags what needs attention.

**Here's the honest answer on why it matters:** as a landlord in England you have a legal duty to make sure the electrical installations in your rental are safe, and to have them inspected and tested at regular intervals by a qualified person. An EICR is how you prove you've done that.

## How to read the codes on your report

When you get your report back, the findings are given codes. These trip a lot of landlords up, so here's what they mean in practice:

- **C1 — Danger present.** There's a risk of injury right now. This needs sorting immediately, and a decent electrician will usually make it safe on the spot.
- **C2 — Potentially dangerous.** Not an immediate risk, but it could become one. This needs remedial work.
- **C3 — Improvement recommended.** Not dangerous, but it would be better brought up to modern standards. You don't have to act on these to get a satisfactory outcome, but they're worth knowing about.
- **FI — Further investigation required.** Something needs a closer look before a verdict can be given.

A report comes back as **satisfactory** or **unsatisfactory**. If you see any C1, C2 or FI codes, it's unsatisfactory, and the flagged work needs doing before you've properly met your obligations.

## What tends to show up in older Gloucester properties

Gloucester and the surrounding villages have a lot of older housing stock, and the rental market is full of it. Period terraces, converted houses, solid-wall cottages. These properties often hide a few recurring electrical surprises.

**The usual suspects I find:**

- **Old rubber or fabric wiring** that has gone brittle with age and needs replacing.
- **Outdated consumer units** with rewireable fuses instead of modern breakers and RCD protection.
- **Poor or missing earthing and bonding**, especially around older plumbing in kitchens and bathrooms.
- **Previous DIY additions** where extra sockets or lights have been added over the decades without proper testing.
- **Signs of overloading** on circuits that were never designed for modern appliance loads.

None of this means an older property is a lost cause. It just means it needs careful, experienced handling rather than a tick-box visit. The same care I'd give the fabric of an old building applies to its wiring.

## How to prepare the property for inspection

A little prep makes the visit faster and cheaper, because the electrician isn't stuck waiting around.

**Before the appointment:**

- Let tenants know the power may go off for short periods during testing.
- Make sure there's clear access to the consumer unit, sockets and the meter.
- Flag any known issues up front, like a socket that's stopped working or a breaker that keeps tripping.
- Have details of any recent electrical work handy if you can.

The clearer the access, the smoother the day, and the sooner your tenants have their kettle back on.

## Common questions

### How often does a rental need an EICR?

The inspection and testing needs to happen at regular intervals as part of your duty as a landlord, and typically a report will state when the next inspection is due. I'll always tell you the recommended next date on your report so nothing lapses by accident.

### My report came back unsatisfactory. What now?

Don't panic. It means there are coded items to address. We'll talk you through exactly what each one is, what it costs, and what genuinely needs doing versus what's a nice-to-have. Once the required work is complete, the installation can be confirmed as satisfactory.

### Can the same inspection be used when I sell or remortgage?

An EICR gives a clear picture of the installation's condition, which is useful information to have on hand. For specific legal or lending requirements, check with your solicitor or lender, but having a recent, clean report rarely hurts.

### Do you only do EICRs, or the remedial work too?

### What if a tenant reports an urgent electrical fault between inspections?

Flag it straight away. A flickering light or a tripping breaker can be a symptom of something that shouldn't wait. We'd rather take a quick look than let it turn into a C1.
