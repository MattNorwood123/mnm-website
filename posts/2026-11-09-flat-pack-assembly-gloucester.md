## Why flat-pack goes wrong (even for handy people)

Most people can follow the pictures. The trouble is that the pictures assume everything else is perfect, and in a real house it rarely is.

**The usual culprits:**

- **Uneven floors.** Older Gloucester terraces and period homes almost never have a dead-level floor. Build a tall wardrobe on sloping boards and the doors drift open on their own.
- **Soft or hollow walls.** Anything that needs fixing back to the wall, like a bookcase or a chest of drawers, needs the right fixing for what's behind the plaster. Get that wrong and it's not just wonky, it's unsafe.
- **Over-tightening.** Chipboard and MDF don't forgive. Crank a cam bolt too hard and it strips, and now the whole panel is loose.
- **Building in the wrong order.** Skip ahead one step and you'll often find you've boxed yourself in and have to take half of it apart again.

None of this is rocket science. It's just the boring stuff before the pretty stuff, and it's exactly the bit people skip when they're tired and want the job done.

## How I build flat-pack so it lasts

When I turn up to a job, I don't just rip the boxes open and start. A bit of discipline up front saves the headache later. Our team is ex-military trained, and that habit of checking before charging in genuinely pays off with flat-pack.

**My order of play:**

1. **Lay everything out and count it.** Panels, fixings, dowels, the lot. Finding a missing part now beats finding it at step 14.
2. **Check the floor and wall.** A quick spirit level tells me whether I need to pack out a foot or find a stud before anything goes together.
3. **Build on a protected surface.** Cardboard or a blanket down so I'm not scratching your new floor or the furniture itself.
4. **Hand-start every fixing before tightening.** It keeps the panels square and stops anything stripping.
5. **Fix tall and heavy items to the wall properly.** Right plug, right screw, into something that'll hold. This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that matters most if you've got kids.
6. **Level the finished piece and adjust the doors** so they shut flush and stay shut.

It's not glamorous. It's just the right way round, and it's why the thing still looks good a year later instead of sagging in the corner.

## Flat-pack in older Gloucester homes

If you're in a period or solid-wall property, flat-pack brings a couple of extra wrinkles worth knowing.

- **Floors are rarely level.** Expect to pack out feet on wardrobes and dressers. It's normal, not a fault.
- **Walls vary wildly.** Some are lath and plaster, some solid masonry, some stud. Each needs a different fixing, and guessing is how shelves end up on the floor.
- **Skirting and picture rails** can stop a tall unit sitting flush to the wall, which throws the fixing points. A small scribe or spacer sorts it, but you need to spot it before you build, not after.

Knowing the local housing stock means I'm not surprised by any of this. It's just part of the job round here.
