### The short version: permission vs permitted development

Not every extension needs a full planning application. In England, some smaller projects can go ahead under what's called **permitted development**, which is essentially a set of pre-approved rights that let you build within certain limits without applying for planning permission.

The catch is that those limits are specific, and they change depending on your property and where it sits. So the real question isn't "do extensions need permission?" It's "does *my* extension, on *my* house, in *my* part of Gloucester, fall inside permitted development or not?"

That's the bit worth getting right before any bricks arrive.

**Two things to understand up front:**

- Permitted development is not the same as "no rules apply." There are still size, height, position and materials conditions to meet.
- Building Regulations are separate from planning permission. Even a project that needs no planning permission usually still needs to meet Building Regs for structure, insulation, drainage and safety.

### When you're more likely to need full planning permission

Every job is different, but these are the situations where I'd expect a proper application rather than relying on permitted development. Ask yourself these before anything else:

1. **Is your home in a conservation area?** Parts of Gloucester and several surrounding villages have conservation designations, and permitted development rights are often tighter or removed there.
2. **Is it a listed building?** Listed properties come with their own consent requirements, and those are strict.
3. **Is it a flat or maisonette?** Permitted development rights for houses generally don't apply in the same way to flats.
4. **Are you going big?** Larger, taller, or two-storey rear extensions, or anything extending towards a road, are far more likely to need permission.
5. **Have the rights already been used or removed?** Some properties have had permitted development rights taken away by a planning condition, and some previous owners have already "used up" the allowance with an earlier extension.

If you tick any of those boxes, don't assume. Check.

### Why older Gloucester homes need an extra bit of thought

We've got a lot of solid-wall period properties round here, from city terraces to older village housing stock. These need careful, experienced handling, and that matters for extensions in two ways.

First, the **planning and design side**. Older and character properties are more likely to sit in conservation areas or be subject to extra scrutiny over how an extension looks from the street.

Second, the **build side**. Tying a modern extension onto an old solid-wall house without thinking about how the two parts breathe is where people store up damp problems for later. Period walls often want materials suited to their age, like lime-based products rather than dense modern cement that traps moisture. That's the boring stuff before the pretty stuff, and it's exactly the sort of detail a stranger rushing a job tends to skip.

### How to find out for certain

Here's the sensible order I'd follow:

- **Check your property's status.** Find out whether you're in a conservation area or listed, and whether any planning conditions apply to your home.
- **Confirm the current rules with the council.** Planning rules and permitted development limits are set nationally and interpreted locally, so your local planning authority is the place to confirm what applies to your specific address and project.
- **Consider a Lawful Development Certificate.** If you believe your extension is permitted development, you can apply for a certificate that formally confirms it. It's a tidy way to have proof for the future, especially when you come to sell.
- **Get a professional eye on it early.** A quick, honest conversation before you design anything can stop you drawing up plans that were never going to fly.

### Common questions

**Does permitted development mean I can build whatever I want?**
No. It means certain projects can proceed without a full planning application, but only within set limits on size, height and position, and only if your rights haven't been restricted or already used.

**Is planning permission the same as Building Regulations?**
No, they're separate. Planning is about whether you're allowed to build and how it looks and affects others. Building Regulations are about how it's built and that it's safe and sound. Most extensions need to satisfy Building Regs even when planning permission isn't required.

**My house is in a conservation area. Does that change things?**
Usually yes. Permitted development rights are often reduced in conservation areas, so you're more likely to need permission and more likely to face design scrutiny. Confirm your status before assuming anything.

**Should I get a Lawful Development Certificate even if I'm sure it's permitted development?**
It's a sensible safeguard. It gives you formal proof that the work was lawful, which is handy if a neighbour queries it or when you sell the property.
