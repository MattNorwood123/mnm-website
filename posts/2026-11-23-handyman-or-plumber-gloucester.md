## Where handyman work ends and plumbing starts

There's a grey area between a quick odd job and a proper plumbing repair, and knowing which side you're on saves you a headache.

As a rough rule: if the job stays on the *visible* side of the pipework and doesn't involve cutting into supply pipes, gas, or your heating system, it's usually handyman territory. The moment you're joining pipes, draining systems, or touching anything to do with gas or pressurised hot water, you want someone who does that work day in, day out.

That's not me being cagey. It's the honest answer, because a bodged pipe joint behind a wall is the kind of thing that turns a cheap job into an expensive one.

## Small plumbing jobs a handyman can sort

Plenty of everyday water niggles don't need a full plumbing visit. These are the sort of tasks I'll happily knock out on a handyman call:

- **Swapping tap washers or cartridges** to stop a dripping tap
- **Replacing a tired kitchen or bathroom tap** on existing isolation valves
- **Re-sealing around a sink, bath or shower tray** when the silicone's gone black and peeling
- **Fitting a new toilet seat, cistern parts, or a float valve** that won't stop running
- **Clearing a slow sink or basin trap** that's blocked with gunk
- **Plumbing in a washing machine or dishwasher** where the valves are already there
- **Replacing a flexible hose or a leaking trap** under a basin

These are the kind of **boring-but-useful fixes** that stop a small drip becoming a warped cupboard base or a stained ceiling downstairs. If you've got a list of them building up, it often makes sense to batch them into one visit rather than ringing round for each.

## Jobs that need a proper plumber, not a quick fix

Some things look simple from the outside and really aren't. Here's where I'll tell you straight that it needs dedicated plumbing work:

- **Anything on your boiler, central heating or hot water cylinder**
- **Moving or adding pipework** for a new kitchen, bathroom or extension
- **Mains supply pipe repairs** or anything under real pressure
- **Hidden or persistent leaks** inside walls, under floors or behind units
- **Full bathroom and kitchen plumbing** as part of a renovation

That last point matters in a lot of Gloucester homes. In older terraces and solid-wall period properties, the pipework has often been patched by three different people over fifty years, so what starts as "just move the sink" can open up a bigger picture. A room that stays damp usually has a ventilation or waterproofing problem sitting behind it, and no amount of new tiling hides that if the real cause gets ignored.

## A quick checklist before you call

Ask yourself these before anything else, because the answers help me help you faster:

1. **Where's the water coming from?** A tap, a pipe joint, the base of the toilet, or somewhere you can't see?
2. **Is it clean water or waste?** Drips from a waste trap are different from mains pressure leaks.
3. **Can you isolate it?** If there's a valve on the pipe, turning it off buys you time.
4. **Has it happened before?** A repeat leak in the same spot usually points to a bigger underlying issue.
5. **Is it near anything electrical?** If water's getting near sockets or your fuse board, treat it as urgent and switch off at the mains.

Even a quick photo on your phone before you ring tends to save a trip, because I can often tell what parts to bring.

## Common questions

### Can a handyman legally do plumbing work?

General plumbing like changing taps, washers, seals and traps is fine for a competent handyman. Gas work is different and must be done by a Gas Safe registered engineer, so anything touching your boiler or gas supply is off-limits for standard handyman work.

### Is it cheaper to get a handyman or a plumber?

For small, visible jobs a handyman call is usually the more sensible spend. For anything involving pipework changes or your heating, paying for proper plumbing first time is cheaper than fixing a leak that shows up weeks later as a cracked tile or a stained ceiling.

### My tap keeps dripping even after I tightened it. What now?

That's almost always a worn washer or cartridge inside, not something tightening will cure. It's a quick swap, and worth sorting before the constant drip wears the valve seat and makes a bigger job of it.

### Should I bundle small plumbing jobs together?

Yes. If you've got a running toilet, a dripping tap and some tired sealant, grouping them into one visit is far better value than booking each separately. It's the same thinking a lot of property managers use when they send us a maintenance list.
